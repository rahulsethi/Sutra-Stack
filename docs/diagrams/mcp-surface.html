The MCP surface — 13 reads, 1 write

Hand-rolled JSON-RPC 2.0 over stdio, zero external dependencies, so a user auditing the thing that decides what their AI can see reads ~700 lines and nothing else. No tool accepts a ceiling, tier, scope, override or passphrase — a test walks every published schema on every build.

Zero dependencies is a trust claim

  • The protocol is implemented directly rather than through an SDK.
  • A supply chain is a poor foundation for a claim about what your AI can see.

What was dropped

  • override_unlock — it wrote a short-TTL flag that raised the ceiling for subsequent calls.
  • A time-boxed ambient flag means a later request is answered at a ceiling nobody authorised for it, which makes “who asked for this?” unanswerable.

A refusal is legible

  • A withheld read returns withheld, not not_found — collapsing them would make a refusal indistinguishable from a typo.
  • It also leaves a reviewable exposure request, so the refusal is actionable rather than mysterious.
